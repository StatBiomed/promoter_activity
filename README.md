# A cross-species atlas of alternative promoter usage during vertebrate organ development

This repository contains the analysis and plotting scripts for a study of alternative promoter usage across seven organs in six vertebrate species: human, rhesus macaque, mouse, rat, rabbit, and chicken. The developmental atlas uses 1,658 RNA-seq libraries spanning embryonic and postnatal stages.

## Guide to the figures

| Figure | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| [Figure 1](./figure1/) | **Developmental promoter atlas.** Compares RNA-seq estimates of promoter activity with CAGE data and examines how promoter usage varies across organs and developmental stages. |
| [Figure 2](./figure2/) | **Major and minor promoters.** Uses human testis to compare the evolutionary ages, genomic locations, and CpG-island overlap of major and minor promoters, and examines how often their major or minor status is shared across species. |
| [Figure 3](./figure3/) | **Developmental changes in alternative promoter activity.** Groups mouse liver promoters by their activity trajectories, examines the functions of their associated genes, and illustrates opposing activity changes at two *Lsp1* promoters. |
| [Figure 4](./figure4/) | **HNF4A and E2F4 in mouse liver.** Relates TF expression, ChIP-seq binding, and sequence motifs to paired promoters with contrasting developmental trajectories, and compares their relative usage in wild-type and *Hnf4a*-knockout liver. |
| [Figure 5](./figure5/) | **Promoter activity and sequence models across species.** Compares the activity of the most active non-internal promoter of each single-copy orthologous gene across conditions, evaluates DNA sequence models, and compares the motifs they identify across species. |
| [Figure 6](./figure6/) | **Stage-specific promoter motifs in mouse liver.** Selects promoters by their predicted activity at each stage relative to other stages, uses integrated gradients and TF-MoDISco to identify sequence motifs, and compares motif clusters across development and against reference motifs. |
